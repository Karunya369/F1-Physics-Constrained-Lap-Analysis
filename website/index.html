01 • Scientific Motivation

Research Problem & Objectives

In Formula 1 broadcast graphics and engineering analysis, theoretical "ideal" laps are routinely computed by summing the fastest observed mini-sector times across teammates. This investigation evaluates the physical validity of that construct.

Core Research Question
"When the fastest segments of different Formula 1 drivers are combined into a theoretical lap, how much of the resulting time advantage remains physically achievable when vehicle-state continuity and forward vehicle-dynamics constraints are enforced?"

The Boundary Discontinuity Problem

A naive theoretical composite selects the minimum elapsed time in each segment:
T_theoretical = ∑ min(dt_{k, A}, dt_{k, B}). However, Driver A may exit segment k at 285 km/h, while Driver B's entry speed for segment k+1 was 302 km/h. Splicing these segments implies instantaneous velocity step changes (Δv) requiring infinite acceleration and unphysical energy injection (ΔKE).

Experimental Setup & Controls

Using official FIA timing feeds via FastF1 from the 2023 Bahrain GP Qualifying (Q3), we analyze the Scuderia Ferrari SF-23 driven by Charles Leclerc (LEC, 89.921 s) and Carlos Sainz (SAI, 90.110 s) on a canonical spatial domain (S = 5369.0 m, 1.0 m PCHIP resampling).

Key Scientific Contributions

1. Systematic audit of boundary state discontinuities (Δv, ΔKE) in empirical composites.
2. Implementation of a macroscopic Level-2 coupled vehicle model with friction ellipse limits.
3. Periodic bisection solver enforcing C0 speed continuity and cyclic boundary conditions.
4. Diagnostic attribution of the feasibility penalty.

Investigation Pipeline

04 • Mathematical Formulation

Methodology & Vehicle Dynamics

Detailed formulation of the Level-2 macroscopic point-mass vehicle dynamics model, friction ellipse coupling, and the forward-backward periodic solver.

1. Point-Mass Equations of Motion

The vehicle of mass m = 810 kg is modeled as a point mass governed by forward acceleration along distance s:

Longitudinal Dynamics
m • v • (dv/ds) = F_x - F_drag - F_rr
F_drag = k_drag • v²
k_drag = k_drag,off • (1 - 0.30 • DRS)

Power constraint: F_traction ≤ P_max / v, with P_max = 740.0 kW (hybrid powertrain limit).

2. Aerodynamic Normal Load

Total normal load F_z combines vehicle static weight and quadratic aerodynamic downforce:

Aerodynamic Normal Load
F_z = m • g + F_down
F_down = k_down • v²
(g = 9.80665 m/s²)

Calibrated values (Policy A): k_down = 2.6185 N/(m/s)², k_drag,off = 1.2500 N/(m/s)².

3. Coupled Friction Ellipse

Longitudinal force F_x and lateral cornering force F_y = m • v² • |κ| are constrained by the tire friction ellipse:

Tire Grip Limit
(F_x / (μ_x • F_z))² + (m • v² • κ / (μ_y • F_z))² ≤ 1

Maximum cornering speed envelope:
v_corner,max(s) = √[ μ_y • g / ( |κ(s)| • (1 - μ_y • k_down / m) ) ]

Calibration Policy A: Literature Baseline (μ_x = 1.7000 fixed)

Public FastF1 telemetry does not provide brake line hydraulic pressures, individual wheel angular speeds, powertrain inertia, or suspension damper velocities. When an unconstrained optimizer runs across telemetry, it reduces longitudinal friction to an unphysical μ_x = 0.9356 to absorb unmodelled braking transients.

Under Policy A, longitudinal tire friction is strictly fixed at its literature reference value (μ_x = 1.7000), while aerodynamic and lateral parameters [k_drag, k_down, μ_y] are calibrated via bounded L-BFGS-B on Charles Leclerc's lap.

06 • Engineering Takeaways

Conclusion & Future Work

Synthesis of findings, engineering significance for motorsport analytics, identified limitations, and roadmap for future vehicle-dynamics extensions.

Scientific Conclusions

1. Theoretical best laps are physically unattainable: The naive mini-sector composite generates an illusion of performance (+1.053 s) by relying on unphysical velocity step jumps (up to 11.70 km/h) and injecting 832.24 kJ of boundary kinetic energy.

2. Feasibility penalty exceeds theoretical advantage: Enforcing speed continuity under empirical donor ceilings adds +2.714 s, producing a feasible lap of 91.583 s (1.661 s slower than Charles Leclerc's observed baseline).

3. Robustness across discretization: Sensitivity sweeps (50m, 100m, 250m) preserve the qualitative finding in every case (Retained fraction R < 0).

Methodological Limitations

1. Macroscopic Point-Mass Model: Omits suspension roll/pitch kinematics, chassis compliance, and dynamic tire load transfer.

2. Public Telemetry Boundaries: FastF1 data lacks steering wheel angle, brake line pressure, tire core/surface temperatures, and battery state-of-charge.

3. Donor-Cap Ceiling Interpretation: The donor cap acts as an empirical feasibility envelope based on demonstrated human driving, not an optimal control trajectory.

Future Research Horizons

1. Multi-Body Vehicle Dynamics: Integrate a four-wheel chassis model with suspension kinematics and aero-map pitch sensitivity.

2. Thermal & Degradation Models: Incorporate transient tire bulk and surface temperature effects into the friction ellipse.

3. Optimal Control Synthesis: Solve minimum-time optimal control directly via collocation methods rather than empirical piecewise donor capping.

4. Multi-Circuit Generalization: Expand validation to high-downforce (Silverstone, Spa) and low-downforce (Monza) circuits.
